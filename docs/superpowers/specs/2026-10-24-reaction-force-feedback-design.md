# Measured reaction-force grasp control

User accepted on 2026-10-24: increase pressure when measured reaction force is insufficient or the object slips during lift; hold once stable, retain a force cap. Replace the 2.3 s automatic lift with measured grasp acquisition and short stability dwell; failure to acquire terminates the episode. No four-pad topology requirement. PPO continues controlling fingers and palm positions.

Add opt-in --force-feedback to adaptive grasp. The final two policy actions set each hand's target sum of the two measured pad/object contact-force magnitudes, 0.1..1.0 N. This is a measured load proxy, not a guarantee of opposing force or successful grasp. Fixed drive stiffness requests use the current 20 Nm/rad ceiling; the 0.2 Nm effort cap remains because diagnostics never approached it. Closure offsets follow calibrated per-hand gap-reducing directions, with rate/position bounds, measured-force filtering, force deadband, excess-force release, and no positive integrator growth while coupling protection is active. Slip only raises the bounded target during lift when measured relative slip is increasing and contact remains. All force-controller state is observed/logged. Old policy contracts remain valid only for old mode.

A grasp can advance close -> hold only with both hands' aggregate pad loads near the requested targets, low object speed and slip; the hold must persist before lift. Losing readiness in hold returns to close. No new requirement on which pads contact. Lift success stays actual bottom >= .33 m, low speed/slip for .5 s. Acquisition times out after 6 s, full episode after 15 s. No force-target reward; reward remains measured contact/height and real success.

Evidence: v35 fixed closure at stiffness20 stayed far below the .2 Nm effort cap; closure was frequently undone by the .004 rad recovery warning. v36 compared stiffness20/40/60/80 with effortcap.8: all four full 15 s episodes safe but no lift; late-close pad loads about .02..04 N. These are diagnostic controllers, not learned policies. Existing 400 s^-1 coupling has a historical random-policy counterexample: any new test is strict and isolated, not assumed safe for training. Never relax .02 rad hard guard or hide recovery.

Verification: synthetic feedback and phase tests; old-mode regressions; serial real Isaac probes with all actions/forces/guards recorded. Start a new PPO run only after physical admission under the exact selected contract. Do not claim stable grasp or generalization without evaluation evidence.

V38 evidence refines readiness: target .1 N yields stable measured hand loads .1007..1088 / .1178..1189 N after 2 s, with load/speed/slip criteria continuously satisfied for 5.63 s at 60 Hz samples. Regulation warnings nevertheless repeatedly reset the dwell. V2 therefore does not use the soft regulation warning as a grasp-state eraser; the unchanged per-substep palm gate, force integrator anti-windup, command recovery and hard guards still apply. This does not authorize palm motion during warnings.

V39 reached lift for the .1 N constant target but then lost contact; the old lift scheduler kept raising empty palms. V3 additionally pauses the scripted vertical lift when either hand load falls below 80% of its requested target or measured slip is increasing above the 2 mm/2 mm-per-second thresholds. Force closure remains active while waiting. No extra downward/root/object pose correction is applied; existing PPO bounded palm controls remain available. Full episode timeout still bounds stalled recovery.

Fresh PPO only initializes the two pressure-head biases to a normalized mean of -.95 (about .1225 N before observation-dependent output), matching the v40 acquisition range .10/.12/.15 N. Other action weights/biases and exploration variance are untouched; exact checkpoint resume never reinitializes the head. Targets and actual loads are also exposed separately in per-environment monitor entries.
